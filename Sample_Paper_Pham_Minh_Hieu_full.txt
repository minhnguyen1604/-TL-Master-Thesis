I. Introduction
1.1. Research Rationales
The relationship between capital structure and firm profitability is one of the most debated questions in corporate finance. Since Modigliani and Miller (1958) established the theoretical baseline, scholars have produced extensive empirical evidence - yet findings remain mixed and context-dependent. In Vietnam, most studies draw national-level samples from listed companies on HoSE or HNX (Doan and Dinh, 2014; Nguyen and Ramachandran, 2006), effectively excluding the non-listed segment that dominates real industrial activity.
Hai Phong hosts over 20 designated industrial and economic zones, including VSIP Hai Phong, Deep C (Dinh Vu – Cat Hai), Nomura, and Trang Due, accommodating hundreds of capital-intensive manufacturing enterprises - many of them foreign-invested and non-listed. These enterprises rely heavily on both short- and long-term bank debt to finance fixed assets and working capital, making the leverage–profitability question particularly relevant. Yet no panel-data study has examined this relationship specifically for this enterprise population.
Furthermore, the 2015–2024 period captures critical shocks - the US-China trade war supply chain relocation (2018–2019), COVID-19 disruption (2020–2021), and post-pandemic FDI recovery - that profoundly affected debt decisions and profitability outcomes. Examining how leverage shaped firm performance across this turbulent decade has direct implications for enterprise managers, lenders, and industrial policy in Hai Phong.
1.2. General Objectives
This thesis examines the impact of financial leverage on the profitability of manufacturing enterprises operating within Hai Phong's Industrial Zones during 2015–2024, using panel data econometric methods. The study aims to provide the first IZ-level quantitative evidence on this relationship in Hai Phong and to generate actionable recommendations for capital structure management.
1.3. Specific Objectives
The following sub-questions will be investigated to answer the general objective:
What are the characteristics of financial leverage and profitability among manufacturing enterprises in Hai Phong Industrial Zones during 2015–2024?
Does short-term debt ratio (STD), long-term debt ratio (LTD), and total debt ratio (TD) have a statistically significant impact on firm profitability (ROA, ROE, ROS)?
What is the direction and magnitude of each leverage component's effect on profitability, and which theoretical framework - trade-off theory or pecking order theory - best explains the results?
What policy and managerial recommendations can be drawn for optimising capital structure in Hai Phong's manufacturing enterprises?
1.4. Thesis Structure
This thesis comprises six chapters. Chapter 1 (Introduction) presents the research rationales, objectives, sub-questions, and overall structure. Chapter 2 (Literature Review) reviews the theoretical frameworks underpinning capital structure decisions - Modigliani-Miller, trade-off theory, pecking order theory, and agency theory - and surveys empirical evidence from international and Vietnamese contexts, identifying the research gap this study addresses. Chapter 3 (Research Context) describes the economic and industrial zone landscape of Hai Phong, providing the institutional background necessary to contextualise the empirical findings. Chapter 4 (Empirical Analysis) details the methodology, data sources, variable definitions, diagnostic tests, and regression results, constituting the core empirical contribution of the thesis. Chapter 5 (Policy Recommendations and Concluding Remarks) interprets the findings, draws implications for enterprise managers, financial institutions, and industrial policymakers, acknowledges limitations, and proposes directions for future research. Chapter 6 (References) lists all cited works in Harvard referencing style.
II. Literature Review
Capital structure theory originates with Modigliani and Miller (1958), who demonstrated that in a perfect capital market, firm value - and hence profitability - is independent of financing mix. The extended 1963 version introduced the corporate tax shield on interest payments, suggesting firms should maximise debt. However, this conclusion contradicts observed firm behaviour and depends on unrealistic assumptions inapplicable to emerging economies such as Vietnam.
The trade-off theory (Kraus and Litzenberger, 1973; Myers, 1984) addresses this by positing that firms balance the tax benefit of debt against expected financial distress costs, arriving at an optimal leverage ratio. For capital-intensive manufacturers in Hai Phong Industrial Zones - with large fixed asset bases providing collateral - trade-off theory predicts a positive relationship between asset tangibility and long-term debt and a positive leverage–profitability link when firms operate below their optimal leverage point.
The pecking order theory (Myers and Majluf, 1984) takes the opposite prediction: information asymmetry between managers and outside investors causes firms to prefer internal funds over debt and debt over equity. The key implication is a negative leverage–profitability relationship - profitable firms accumulate retained earnings and rely less on external debt. This prediction is particularly relevant for Hai Phong's FDI enterprises, which commonly finance operations through intra-group transfers rather than local borrowing.
Agency theory (Jensen and Meckling, 1976; Jensen, 1986) adds that debt can discipline management by constraining free cash flow overinvestment, suggesting a potentially positive leverage–performance link for firms with weak governance. Together, these three theories generate competing predictions that this study tests empirically.
Empirical evidence is mixed. Zeitun and Tian (2007) find a significant negative impact of total debt on ROA in Jordan, consistent with the pecking order. Gill, Biger and Mathur (2011) find a positive relationship for US manufacturers, supporting the trade-off view. Weill (2008) demonstrates that institutional environment substantially moderates the direction of effect across European countries. Within Vietnam, Doan and Dinh (2014) document a negative long-term debt effect on ROE for listed firms; Nguyen and Ramachandran (2006) show that liquidity constrains SME access to long-term credit; Biger, Nguyen and Hoang (2008) confirm that tangibility and firm size are principal capital structure determinants.
The critical gap is that all Vietnamese studies use national listed-firm samples. No study examines the leverage–profitability relationship specifically for non-listed manufacturing enterprises in Hai Phong's Industrial Zones using panel data. This study fills that gap.
III. Empirical Analysis
3.1. Methodology
This study employs a balanced panel data approach, which combines the cross-sectional dimension (enterprise variation) and the time-series dimension (annual variation over 2015–2024) to control for unobserved firm-level heterogeneity and reduce omitted variable bias (Baltagi, 2008). Three parallel regression models are estimated - one for each leverage proxy - to avoid perfect multicollinearity, since total debt is by construction the sum of short- and long-term debt. The general model is:
PROFit = α + β1 LEVit + β2 SIZEit + β3 TANGit + β4 LIQit + αi + γt + εit                                                                        (1)
Where PROFⁱₜ ∈ {ROA, ROE, ROS} is the profitability measure for enterprise i in year t; LEV is the leverage proxy (STD, LTD, or TD across three separate estimations); αᵢ captures time-invariant firm fixed effects; γₜ captures common time shocks; and εᵢₜ is the idiosyncratic error. The three specific models are:
ROAit = α + β1 STDit + β2 SIZEit + β3 TANGit + β4 LIQit + αi + γt + εit                                                                        (2)
ROAit = α + β1 LTDit + β2 SIZEit + β3 TANGit + β4 LIQit + αi + γt + εit                                                                        (3)
ROAit = α + β1 TDit + β2 SIZEit + β3 TANGit + β4 LIQit + αi + γt + εit                                                                        (4)
Models (2)–(4) are replicated with ROE and ROS as dependent variables, yielding nine regressions in total. Variable definitions are summarised in Table 1. The dependent variables capture different performance dimensions: ROA measures asset productivity, ROE measures shareholder returns, and ROS captures operating margin efficiency. The three leverage proxies - STD (short-term debt / total assets), LTD (long-term debt / total assets), and TD (total liabilities / total assets) - are the primary variables of interest. Control variables are firm size (SIZE = ln(total assets)), asset tangibility (TANG = fixed assets / total assets), and liquidity (LIQ = current assets / current liabilities).

The estimation follows a sequential diagnostic protocol (Baltagi, 2008). First, Pooled OLS is run as baseline. The Breusch-Pagan Lagrange Multiplier test then selects between OLS and Random Effects (RE). The Hausman test selects between Fixed Effects (FE) and RE. The Modified Wald test checks for groupwise heteroskedasticity in the FE residuals; the Wooldridge test checks for first-order serial autocorrelation; and the Pesaran CD test checks for cross-sectional dependence. Given that all three problems are expected in manufacturing panel data - as found in the reference thesis - Panel-Corrected Standard Errors (PCSE, Beck and Katz, 1995) are applied as the final robust estimator. All estimations are conducted in Stata 17.
3.2. Data and Pre-Tests
The dataset comprises audited annual financial statements of manufacturing enterprises operating in designated Industrial Zones in Hai Phong City over 2015–2024. Data are sourced from the National Business Registration Portal (dangkykinhdoanh.gov.vn), the Hai Phong Department of Planning and Investment (which holds FDI enterprise audited accounts), and the FiinPro commercial financial database. The author's internship relationship with the Hai Phong Economic Zone Authority (HEZA) facilitates enterprise identification and data access - a primary advantage over purely desk-based studies.
The target sample is 40–60 manufacturing enterprises with at least five consecutive years of data within the 2015–2024 window, yielding 400 – 600 firm-year observations. The sample is restricted to enterprises with positive equity and non-zero revenue to exclude dormant or distressed firms. Winsorisation at the 1st and 99th percentile will be applied to continuous variables to mitigate outlier influence.
Pre-tests include: (i) descriptive statistics and correlation matrix to check for multicollinearity among independent variables; (ii) Variance Inflation Factor (VIF) tests - VIF > 10 indicates problematic collinearity; (iii) panel unit root tests (Im-Pesaran-Shin) to check for stationarity in leverage and profitability series; and (iv) the full diagnostic battery described in Section 3.1. A key data limitation is that non-listed enterprise financial statements are not universally publicly available, which may constrain sample coverage. This will be addressed through direct engagement with HEZA and Hai Phong's Department of Planning and Investment.
3.3. Findings and Interpretation
Based on the theoretical framework and the existing Vietnamese empirical evidence, the following results are anticipated. For the total debt model (equation 4), TD is expected to have a statistically significant negative coefficient on ROA and ROS, consistent with pecking order theory: enterprises with higher total leverage bear greater interest burdens and financial distress costs that erode profitability. This is aligned with Doan and Dinh (2014) and Zeitun and Tian (2007).
For the short-term debt model (equation 2), STD is expected to have a significant negative effect on all three profitability measures, reflecting the liquidity pressure and refinancing risk that characterises short-term borrowing among domestic SMEs in Hai Phong's Industrial Zones. For the long-term debt model (equation 3), the direction of LTD's coefficient is an open empirical question - it may support fixed asset investment (positive) or impose debt service burdens (negative), and the data will determine which force dominates.
Among control variables, SIZE is expected to have a positive coefficient (larger firms benefit from economies of scale), TANG is expected to positively predict leverage access but have a neutral or positive profitability effect, and LIQ is expected to be positively associated with profitability (greater liquidity reduces distress costs). Results will be cross-validated against the findings of the reference thesis (Pham Minh Hieu, 2023) and discussed in light of the three competing theoretical frameworks.
IV. Conclusions
This thesis design proposes the first panel-data investigation of financial leverage and firm profitability for manufacturing enterprises in Hai Phong Industrial Zones over 2015–2024. The study addresses a clear empirical gap - no existing research applies this methodology to the non-listed FIE segment that dominates Hai Phong's industrial economy - using a nine-regression PCSE panel framework across three leverage proxies and three profitability measures. Findings are expected to confirm a negative total-debt–profitability relationship consistent with pecking order theory, with important implications for capital structure management in Vietnam's fastest-growing industrial city. Limitations include the availability of non-listed enterprise financial statements, which will be mitigated through the author's institutional access to HEZA data channels.
